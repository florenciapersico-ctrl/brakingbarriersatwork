# Preguntas Entrevista

Job Interview – Questions & Answers
She Breaks Limits | Breaking Barriers Method | Flor Pérsico
This document contains 17 classic job interview questions with tips, example answers (correct and incorrect), and frameworks to help students practice and perform confidently in English.

1. "Tell Me About Yourself"
Focus on recent experience relevant to the role. Keep it brief, tailored, and backed by achievements.
Tips:
Only talk about recent, relevant experiences
Tailor your answer to the job and industry
Back your claims with achievements

Example #1 – Marketing
"I have been working in marketing for the past 3 years, focusing on social media campaigns and content creation. In my current role, I manage a small team and we've successfully increased engagement for our clients by 20%. Now, I am looking to bring my experience to a bigger company like yours, where I can work on larger campaigns and continue growing in this field."
Example #2 – Recent graduate
"I recently graduated with a degree in business administration, and during my studies, I completed an internship at a logistics company. There, I learned how to manage inventory and track shipments efficiently. I'm excited to apply what I learned to this role and continue improving my skills in operations."

2. "What Is Your Greatest Weakness?"
Choose a weakness that is not critical to the role and show you are actively working to improve it.
Structure:
Name the weakness — be honest but strategic
Explain what you are doing to improve
Show progress if possible

Example #1 – Organization
"One weakness I have is staying organized. Sometimes, my desk and inbox get messy, and this makes me less productive. To fix this, I have started to take some time every day to clean and organize my workspace. This has helped me to work better and faster."
Example #2 – Giving feedback
"I sometimes find it hard to give feedback to my coworkers because I don't want to hurt their feelings. But in my last job, someone asked me to help them improve their work. I learned that feedback can be helpful when you give it in a kind way. Now, I feel more comfortable giving feedback and making it useful."
Example #3 – Self-criticism
"I can be very hard on myself. I want my work to be perfect, but sometimes this makes me stressed. I have learned to take better care of myself and to be more patient with my work. I also learned to see when my inner voice is helpful and when it's not."

3. "What Is Your Greatest Strength?"
Stay humble, stay relevant, and back up your claims with real evidence.
Rules:
Stay humble — don't sound arrogant
Choose a strength relevant to the job
Back it up with a specific example

Correct – Communication skills
"My greatest strength is communication. In my last job as an HR representative, I helped resolve conflicts between employees. I made sure that everyone could talk and share their opinions in a respectful way. This improved teamwork and made the workplace more positive."
Correct – Work under pressure
"One of my greatest strengths is working under pressure. In my last job, I led a team to complete a big project for a client. We had a tight deadline, but by working together and communicating clearly, we finished the project on time. The client was very happy with the result."
Incorrect – Arrogant answer (avoid this)
"Honestly, I'm the best person for the job. In my last job, I outperformed everyone and always fixed the problems. I often corrected my managers because I know more than them."
Incorrect – Too humble (avoid this)
"Well, I'm not sure I have any particular strengths that stand out. I just do my best to meet expectations and support the team."

4. "What Is Your Greatest Achievement?"
Give a specific example using the STAR method. Connect it to skills relevant to the job.
STAR Framework:
S – Situation: Set the context
T – Task: Your specific responsibility
A – Action: What YOU did
R – Result: Measurable outcome

Example – Professional
"In my last job, my biggest achievement was leading a team to finish an important project before the deadline. We had to install new software for the company. I made sure we communicated well and stayed focused, and we finished two weeks early. This helped the company improve efficiency by 15%."
Example – Personal (motherhood)
"My greatest achievement has been raising my children while continuing to develop my professional career. This experience has taught me to manage my time effectively, prioritize tasks, and be resilient in the face of challenges. I've developed strong organizational and multitasking skills, which I believe are valuable for this role."

5. "Why Should We Hire You?"
Show a direct match between your skills and the company's needs. Use specific achievements.
Focus on:
Direct match with the job requirements
Specific achievements with numbers
Enthusiasm to contribute to the company's success

Example #1 – Problem solving
"I'm good at solving problems and quickly adapting to changes. In my last job, I helped improve a social media campaign, and in one month, we increased engagement by 30%. I'm excited to bring my creativity and problem-solving skills to your company."
Example #2 – Leadership
"As a project manager, my main strength is leading teams. I make sure that projects are done on time and on budget. In my last job, I led a team that finished a software project three weeks early, improving performance by 25%."

6. "Do You Prefer Working Individually or With a Team?"
Most employers want candidates who can do both. Express your preference and show flexibility.

Best of both worlds
"I enjoy both teamwork and working independently. Sometimes, it's important to brainstorm with others, but I also like having time to focus on my tasks alone. In my current job, I often switch between team meetings and independent work, which has helped me solve problems in different ways."

7. "Why Do You Want to Work Here?"
Connect your skills and goals to the company's mission and needs.

Example
"I want to join your company because I think it is a great place for me to grow in communication. I have experience in writing, marketing, and creating content, and I believe these skills will help your team. I am excited about your company's innovative approach and the chance to work on projects that align with my passion for impactful communication."

8. "How Do You See Yourself in Five Years?"
Show ambition but also alignment with the role. Employers want commitment, not a stepping stone.

Correct
"In five years, I see myself taking on more responsibility within the company and continuing to contribute to its success. My goal is to become a key player in driving both team and company growth. My immediate focus is excelling in this position and making meaningful contributions."
Incorrect – avoid
"In five years, I see myself having moved on to a bigger company or perhaps starting my own business. I think this role is a great stepping stone."

9. "Why Do You Want to Leave Your Current Job?"
Keep it positive. Focus on growth, not complaints. Never speak negatively about your current employer.

Correct
"I've really enjoyed my time at my current company and have learned a great deal. However, I feel ready to take on new challenges and expand my skills. I'm looking for a role that offers more opportunities for growth, which is why this position is so appealing to me."
Incorrect – avoid
"My boss and I didn't see eye to eye, and I wasn't getting along well with the team. I think a new environment will be a better fit for me."

10. "What Are Your Salary Expectations?"
Give a range based on market research. Leave room for negotiation.

Example
"I'm looking for a fair salary that reflects my experience and what I can offer. Based on what I've learned about the job and the market, I think a salary between $X and $Y would be good. But I'm also open to talking about it more to find the best option for both the company and me."

11. "What Are Your Hobbies & Interests?"
Use this to humanize yourself. Choose a hobby that shows a skill relevant to work.

Example – Sports
"I like playing team sports, and I'm part of a local volleyball team. I'm the captain, so I make sure everyone communicates well and works together. This role has helped me improve my leadership and teamwork skills, which are also important in my job."
Example – Traveling
"I love traveling and plan trips whenever I can. Traveling has taught me to adapt to changes and stay organized. This skill helps me at work too, especially when I have many tasks to finish."

12. "How Do You Handle Stress?"
Show you have a clear strategy and give a real-life example.

Example
"When I feel stressed, I take a moment to stop and plan my tasks. I divide big projects into smaller, easier steps and focus on the most important ones first. This helps me stay organized and calm. For example, in a recent project with short deadlines, I divided the work into smaller tasks and finished them one by one."

13. "Describe Yourself in Three Words"
Choose words connected to the job. Back each one up with a brief example.
Word bank by role type:
Analyst/Tech: Organized, Focused, Analytical, Problem-solver, Methodical, Thorough
Client Facing: Outgoing, Approachable, Friendly, Caring, Trustworthy, Sociable
Sales: Ambitious, Proactive, Determined, Driven, Motivated, Upbeat
Marketing/Creative: Creative, Inventive, Imaginative, Collaborative, Innovative
Corporate: Professional, Diplomatic, Calm, Confident, Adaptable, Conscientious

Example
"I would describe myself as hardworking, communicative, and reliable. I always set goals for myself and work hard to reach them. I like working with others and learning from them, and I'm very reliable when it comes to my responsibilities. If I say I will do something, I will always finish it on time."

14. "What Motivates You at Work?"
Choose something connected to the job. Show genuine enthusiasm.
Ideas:
Solving problems / Learning new things / Working with a team
Taking on challenges / Making a difference / Supporting others

Example
"What really motivates me is making a difference. I like knowing that the work I do has a positive impact on people. In my previous role, I worked closely with clients, and helping them solve their problems always gave me a sense of accomplishment."

15. "What Makes You Unique?"
Don't compare yourself to others. Highlight skills that complement the role.
Strategy:
Review the job description and mention the most requested skills
Add your own unique combination of skills
Connect your uniqueness to the company's needs

Example – Communication skills required
"One thing that makes me unique is my ability to communicate well with different kinds of people. I've worked in customer service and also managed client accounts. This makes me a good fit for the role because I can bring my experience in working with people from different backgrounds."

16. "What's Your Ideal Job?"
Show how this role fits your long-term goals. Don't describe a completely different job.

Example – Related position
"My dream job would let me use my customer service and communication skills. I love helping clients solve their problems and improve their experience. This role would help me keep growing my problem-solving abilities and develop my communication skills."

17. "Do You Have Any Questions for Us?"
NEVER say 'No questions, thank you.' Always ask at least one question. It shows interest and preparation.
Good questions to ask:
What are the main responsibilities of this position?
What does a typical day look like for someone in this role?
What are the opportunities for growth and training within the company?
What challenges might I encounter in this role?
How is performance typically evaluated?
What do you enjoy most about working here?
What are the next steps in the interview process?
